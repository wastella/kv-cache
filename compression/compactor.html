Compactor

TL;DR: Compactor isn't a new scoring idea by itself, it's a wrapper that blends two of them: a score derived purely from the geometry of the key vectors, no attention involved, and a score from a deliberately non-causal attention pass that lets every token look both forward and backward. kvpress ships both halves as their own presses; Compactor just combines them.

Two ingredients, one blend

Chari and Van Durme's Compactor scores every cached key with a weighted sum of two independent signals, computed once during prefill:

Leverage score. Statistical leverage is a classical idea from regression: it measures how much an individual data point would change a least-squares fit if it were removed, i.e. how much of an outlier it is relative to everything else. kvpress's LeverageScorePress approximates this on the pre-RoPE key vectors without ever forming a full sequence-length-by-sequence-length matrix: it right-multiplies the (mean-centered) keys by a small random Gaussian sketch (48 columns by default), builds a tiny Gram matrix from that sketch, and solves it with a Cholesky decomposition to recover an approximate leverage score per token. The scores are z-normalized. Nothing here looks at a query or an attention weight, it's purely about how unusual a key's geometry is.

Non-causal chunked attention. Normal attention during prefill is causal: token i can only attend to tokens at or before it. kvpress's NonCausalAttnPress deliberately breaks that rule for scoring purposes only. It tiles the sequence into chunks (256 tokens by default), and inside each chunk lets every query attend to every key, forward and backward, then sums up how much attention each key receives from all the queries in its chunk. That sum gets weighted by the key's own value-vector norm, lightly smoothed, and z-normalized. It's a real attention-based score, just not a causal one: a key can be marked important because a query before it in the sequence would want to attend to it, something no causal method can see during prefill.

Compactor's score() then computes blending × leverage_z + attention_z. That's worth reading carefully: it isn't a weighted average of the two, the attention term always keeps full weight and blending only scales the leverage term up or down. By default blending is set equal to compression_ratio, which the docstring calls a good heuristic default rather than something derived from the paper's math. The first sink_size_start (8 by default) and last sink_size_end (4) tokens get their combined score overwritten with the maximum score among the rest, so they'll typically survive, but that's a soft nudge, not an absolute guarantee: if the compression budget ends up smaller than the total number of sink positions, some of them still get pruned, they're just tied for the top of the ranking rather than exempt from it. The press only supports prefill; it asserts on this and will fail loudly if you try it mid-decode.

Where this shines, and where it doesn't

Compactor is training-free and query-agnostic: compression happens once at prefill and the result is reusable no matter what the model is later asked about, which matters if the same long context gets queried repeatedly. Combining a purely geometric signal with a genuinely attention-based one (even a non-causal one) gives it something neither signal has alone: leverage catches outlier keys that attention-based methods like SnapKV might miss, while the non-causal attention term catches keys a query would actually want, which a purely geometric method like Knorm can't see. Per the paper's own reported numbers, Compactor matches baseline accuracy while retaining roughly 20% fewer tokens than competing methods, and reports a 68% reduction in KV memory on LongBench.

The costs are specific. It's prefill-only, so it can't keep compressing a cache that's still growing during decode the way DMS or SimLayerKV can. The Cholesky solve and Gaussian sketch avoid ever materializing a full attention matrix, but they're still real extra compute spent at compression time, on top of the forward pass itself. The non-causal attention score is chunked with a hard 256-token boundary by default, so a key's score only reflects what's asking for it within its own chunk, nothing outside that window plays a role. And as above, sink protection is a ranking nudge, not a hard floor: at extreme compression ratios, even sink tokens compete with each other for a shrinking budget.

Real implementation

kvpress implements this as CompactorPress, a wrapper that instantiates and calls the score() methods of LeverageScorePress and NonCausalAttnPress directly, then blends and pads the results. See the source: compactor_press.py, leverage_press.py, non_causal_attention_press.py. Original paper: Chari, Van Durme, "Compactor: Calibrated Query-Agnostic KV Cache Compression with Approximate Leverage Scores," 2025.
