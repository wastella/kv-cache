H2O — Heavy-Hitter Oracle

Instead of guessing from position alone, watch which tokens actually earn attention as generation proceeds, and keep the ones that keep earning it. A small number of "heavy hitters" tend to account for most of the attention mass — everyone else is a safer bet to evict.

The heavy-tailed observation

Zhang et al. measured attention scores accumulated across generation steps and found the distribution is highly skewed: a small subset of tokens consistently receive a large share of total attention, regardless of their position in the sequence. Those tokens — heavy hitters — tend to matter for the rest of generation too, because removing them measurably hurts output quality, while removing low-scoring tokens barely registers.

H2O turns that observation into an eviction policy: maintain a running score per cached token (its accumulated attention so far), and whenever the cache needs to shrink, drop the lowest-scoring tokens outside a small recent window. It's a greedy, content-aware alternative to StreamingLLM's fixed rule.

Try it

Darker tokens have higher simulated cumulative attention. Raise the compression ratio and watch the lowest scorers — not the oldest tokens — go first.

Simulated, not measured. A real H2O implementation accumulates these scores from actual attention weights during a model's forward pass. This demo generates plausible-looking scores locally in the browser (a recency term plus a few random "heavy hitter" spikes) so you can see the eviction logic clearly — the numbers themselves aren't from a real model.

Where this shines, and where it doesn't

H2O adapts to content: a token that turns out to matter gets to stay, wherever it sits in the sequence, which fixes StreamingLLM's blind spot for important mid-context information. The cost is that scoring isn't free — you need to track and update attention statistics for every cached token, which adds memory and compute overhead of its own. And those statistics only reflect what's happened so far: an early attention score can be a noisy predictor of long-run importance, since a token might look unimportant at first and matter a lot later.

Real implementation

kvpress implements this family as ObservedAttentionPress, which scores tokens by their average observed attention weight during prefill. See the source: observed_attention_press.py. Original paper: Zhang et al., "H2O: Heavy-Hitter Oracle for Efficient Generative Inference of Large Language Models," 2023.
